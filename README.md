# Re-Scoring of the Open Class - 40th World Gliding Championships, Poland 2026
[Original Scores can be found here...](https://www.soaringspot.com/en_gb/wgc2026/results/open)

With the recent upset (or lack thereof) in the final result for the Open class at the @<40th wgc>, there have been many ‘what-if’ theories circulating -

Some common examples would be **“_What if the JS3 (18m class glider) did not fly in the Open class?_”** - and alternatives like “_What if the last placed glider was discounted from scoring each day?_”. Even more extreme experiments would be to re-score the competition using GP scoring (as some have already done) - but that would not be a completely relevant approach. The scoring system is largely seen as “black magic” by many who do not understand it fully - and they do have a point - it is an extremely complicated algorithm.

__With that in mind, I had decided to re-score the competition according to the standard IGC Annex A scoring, but exclude the 18m-class glider from scoring.__  
This is, of course, purely hypothetical - but it does bring to light some interesting effects in the scoring system. 

The most obvious, and widely discussed, is that the final rankings would have changed - and **YY would have claimed the title of World Champion.**

The bigger picture is slightly more nuanced, though - by (effectively) disqualifying the glider in question - what was the actual impact on the scores as a whole? With a good understanding of the Annex A scoring system, one could easily predict that each day would be de-valued slightly less - since a larger proportion of pilots would have completed the task, so speed would account for more points, and the points difference between the top pilots should increase.

Over the duration of a long competition, this theoretically shouldn’t change the final rankings - but only cause the winners to be separated by more points. But in reality, this is clearly not the case - as the recalculation has proved that the rankings did, in fact, change - so why is that?

With a glider that is clearly not performing in the same league as the majority of the class, the winners scores are largely influenced by luck: whether or not the last placed glider finished the task on a given day (or, more precisely, flew faster than 2/3 of the winning speed). If not, the day becomes devalued, and the winning scores are pulled closer together.
  This effect becomes even more dramatic on days where there is a major difference in the winners speeds (ie. Task 10 at the WGC) - if the day became devalued, there were less points awarded for speed, and so the top scorers were separated by less. Sebastian needed to be awarded the maximum possible speed points, to increase his lead. Unfortunately in the case of this competition, the points were already so close, that this (almost) random event of whether the last placed glider flew faster than 90kph, was enough to change the winner of the World Championship. And it did.

_So why does this matter? Should we change the scoring system? What about the rules?_
I’d first like to make it clear that i don’t believe the final results should have been changed in any way - and the end result was completely fair, considering the field in the competition. Felipe Levin is undeniably the World Champion. 
With that said, it does naturally lead to the discussion that perhaps changing the scoring system is not the right thing to do - but changing the sampling system is. Perhaps there should be a fixed qualifying criteria for WGC participation - or, simpler to implement, the last placed competitor on each day should be discarded from scoring (for that day only). I’m not suggesting that either of these are appropriate measures, but they are undoubtedly worth considering - and maybe due to recent events, people will give these things more thought.

All in all, I believe it was the most exciting WGC I have followed - and the performance from @<Sebastian> was an absolute masterpiece. It’s a pity the result wasn’t different; but there’s always next time.

Laurence Hardman
